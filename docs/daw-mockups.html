DAW, its own tool in Buddy

Three ways to lay out the tool that grows out of the Keys tab. Each one is a full Buddy window, sitting in the sidebar right after Audio Assistant. Every knob, fader, pad and key below can be dragged or clicked, and Play runs the playhead. Everything here is made-up example content in A minor at 124 BPM.

Where things live

Pick a sound on the left and drag it onto a track. Click a track to edit its instrument on the right. The mixer is always visible at the bottom.

The song lane

The music you picked in Resolve sits in the first lane, with its bars as the grid, so everything you add lines up with the real song.

Best for

Building a full cue with several parts: chords, bass, drums and a lead over the song.

Direction B

Console

One instrument at a time, built like a piece of hardware: a faceplate of knobs with a live scope, a large piano roll you edit directly, and the keyboard with pitch and mod wheels. It feels the most like an instrument and the least like software.

Editing notes

Drag a note to move it and drag its right edge to make it longer. Velocity sits right under the notes. In-key rows are shaded, and the key's root is warmer.

The scope

Shows the filter as you turn Cutoff and Reso, so each knob's effect is visible as well as audible.

Best for

Shaping one sound and writing one part carefully: a melody, a bass line, a chord bed.

Direction C

Perform

Made for getting an idea down fast without music theory: chord pads already in the song's key, four big macro knobs and an XY pad that each move several settings at once, and a drum step sequencer. Every pad press can be recorded.

Macros

Brightness opens the filter and adds drive. Space adds room and echo together. Motion adds vibrato and flutter. Punch shortens the attack and adds compression. The XY pad moves two of them at once.

Chord pads

Each pad is a chord that belongs to the song's key, labelled with its numeral. You can't play a wrong chord.

Best for

Editors who want a bed, a drum loop or a stinger quickly and don't play keyboard.

Recommendation

What I'd build

  1. Arrange (A) as the main view. It holds every feature without hiding anything, and the song lane keeps every part lined up with the real cue.
  2. Console's faceplate (B) as the instrument editor. It opens full size when you double-click a track, with the scope and piano roll.
  3. Perform (C) as a mode you switch to. It's the quickest way in for someone who doesn't play keyboard.
  4. Build it in order: the tool and its tracks with the Keys features moved over, then knobs and the mixer, then the drum machine, then the piano roll editing, then Perform.
